Rocket Launch:
Design Mission 🚀

Measure a real rocket launcher, design a rocket that fits it, 3D print it, and launch it!

Your mission

Our stomp launcher shoots a puff of air up a launch tube. A rocket slides down over the tube, and when you stomp, the air blasts it into the sky. You’ll design your own rocket in TinkerCad that fits our launcher. Nobody will tell you the sizes. You’ll measure them yourself, like a real engineer!

Our launcher
We use a stomp rocket launcher: you stomp on an air pad, and a hose carries the air to an upright launch tube. The foam rockets it comes with slide down over that tube. Ours is this one on Amazon, but any stomp launcher with a launch tube works. Just measure your tube!
Mission rules: your rocket must…
  1. Slide onto the launch tube easily, but not wobble around loosely.
  2. Have a closed top, so the air pushes the rocket up instead of shooting out the top.
  3. Have fins, so it flies straight.
  4. Be light: thin walls, no solid chunks. Lighter rockets fly higher!
  5. Be named Rocket – First Name Last Initial in TinkerCad, so your teacher can find it and print it.

Words to know

Calipers
A tool that measures small things very exactly, down to a hundredth of a millimeter.
Diameter
How wide a circle is, measured straight across through the middle.
Gap (clearance)
A little extra space, so one part can slide over another without getting stuck.

📏 Meet the calipers

How to use them

  1. Press the on button. Make sure the screen says mm (not inches). If it doesn’t, press the mm/in button.
  2. Close the jaws all the way, then press ZERO. The screen should say 0.00.
  3. Open the jaws, put the object in, and close them gently. Don’t squeeze! Squeezing gives the wrong number.
  4. Read the screen. Write down all the numbers, like 21.45 mm.

Three ways to measure

Outside
Squeeze the big bottom jaws gently around it.
Inside
Put the small top jaws in the hole, then open them until they touch.
Depth
Rest the end on the edge and slide the rod down to the bottom.

Why add a gap?
If the hole is exactly the size of the tube, the rocket gets stuck. 3D printed holes also come out a tiny bit smaller than designed. A gap of about 0.8 mm is a good start.
Why add 2 walls?
The wall goes all the way around, so it’s on both sides of the hole when you measure straight across.
Check your plan
B is bigger than A ☐   C is about 3 mm bigger than B ☐   E is bigger than D ☐   E + F is under 150 mm ☐

1Get set up

  1. Open TinkerCad and click Create → 3D Design.
  2. Name it right away! Click the silly random name at the top left, type Rocket – First Name Last Initial, and press Enter.
Typing exact sizes
Click a shape, hover over a white square, click the number that pops up, and type your size from page 4 (decimals like 23.8 are fine!). Press Enter.

2The body

  1. Drag a Cylinder onto the workplane.
  2. Make it C wide, C long, and E tall.

3The hole

  1. Drag another Cylinder next to the body. Click Hole .
  2. Make it B wide, B long, and D tall. Leave it sitting on the workplane, so the hole opens at the bottom.
  3. Select the hole and the body (hold Shift and click both). Click Align and click the two middle dots that line things up side to side. Don’t click the up-and-down row!

4The nose cone

  1. Drag a Paraboloid (rounded) or a Cone (pointy) onto the workplane. Which one do you think will fly better?
  2. Make it C wide, C long, and F tall.
  3. Grab the black cone on top and lift it to E, so it sits right on top of the body.
  4. Select the nose and the body, Align the two middle dots again.
Check
Click the view cube’s Home button. You should see one smooth rocket shape, with the nose sitting right on top of the body (not floating, not sunk in).

5The fins

Here’s a trick: one long, thin box sticking out on both sides of the rocket makes two fins at once!

  1. Drag a Box onto the workplane. Make it C + 30 long, 2 wide (that’s the fin thickness), and 30 tall.
  2. Select the box and the body, Align the two middle dots. Now it pokes out 15 mm on each side.
  3. With just the box selected, press Ctrl + D (Duplicate). Grab the curved arrow on the flat part, near the bottom, and turn the copy 90°. Now you have 4 fins!
Engineer’s choice
Change the size of your fins, or try the Wedge shape for slanted fins. Bigger fins fly straighter, but they’re heavier. What will you choose? Keep fins at least 1.5 mm thick so they don’t snap.

6Group it

  1. Press Ctrl + A to select everything, then click Group (Ctrl + G).
  2. Turn the view cube to look at the rocket from underneath. You should see the round hole. The hole also cleans the fins out of the inside!

7Save it and hand it in 💾

  1. Check the name at the top left says Rocket – First Name Last Initial.
  2. TinkerCad saves by itself. To be sure, click the TinkerCad logo (top left) and find your rocket in your designs.
  3. Write your sizes from page 4 somewhere safe. If your rocket doesn’t fit, you’ll need them to fix it!
  4. Tell your teacher your rocket is ready to print. 🖨️

🚀 Launch day!

Safety rules
  • Never aim the launcher at a person, an animal, or a window. Always point it up!
  • Only stomp when your teacher says “Go!” and everyone is standing back.
  • Wait for a rocket to land before you pick it up.

The fit test

Slide your rocket onto the launch tube. How does it feel? Circle one:

Too tight 😬     Just right 😃     Too loose 🙃

Too tight? Make your gap bigger. Too loose? Make it smaller. Change B and C, then print again.
