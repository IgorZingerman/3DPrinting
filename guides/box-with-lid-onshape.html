Box with a Lid
in OnShape 📦

Design a little box with a lid that actually fits. It’s a real engineering challenge, because two parts have to fit together!

The box and lid cut in half, so you can see inside. (The gap is drawn bigger than it really is.)

The secret: the lip and the gap

The lid has a lip: a thin rim underneath that slides inside the box, so the lid can’t slide off. But if the lip is exactly the size of the inside of the box, it won’t fit! 3D prints come out a tiny bit fat. So we leave a gap of 0.3 mm on every side.

Before you start
Finish the Spinning Top first. Make a new document named Box – First Name Last Initial, and set Workspace units to Millimeter.

1Sketch the box shape

  1. Click Sketch, then click the Top plane.
  2. Click the Center point rectangle tool. Click on the origin first (that’s the center), then click again to make a rectangle.
  3. Click Dimension (D). Click the top line and type 60. Click a side line and type 40.
  4. Click the green ✔ to finish the sketch.

2Pull it up into 3D

  1. Click Extrude. Click inside your rectangle.
  2. Make sure New is chosen at the top. Set Depth to 30 mm. Click ✔.
Check
You have a solid block, 60 × 40 × 30 mm. It’s a brick for now!

3Round the corners

  1. Click Fillet. Click the 4 up-and-down edges at the corners of the block (spin the view to reach the back ones).
  2. Set the radius to 5 mm and click ✔.

4Hollow it out

  1. Click Shell. For Faces to remove, click the top face of the block.
  2. Set Thickness to 2 mm. Click ✔.
Check
Now it’s an open box with 2 mm walls and rounded corners. 🎉 The feature list shows: Sketch 1, Extrude 1, Fillet 1, Shell 1.

5The lid top

We’ll copy the outline of the box’s rim, so the lid matches perfectly.

  1. Click Sketch, then click the top rim of the box (the thin flat edge all the way around).
  2. Click the Use tool (U), then click the rim again. OnShape copies its outside and inside outlines into your sketch. Press Esc, then click ✔.
  3. Click Extrude. Click both shaded areas of the new sketch: the thin rim ring and the big middle.
  4. Choose New at the top (not Add!). Set Depth to 3 mm. If it goes down into the box, click the flip arrow so it goes up. Click ✔.
Check
The Parts list now says Parts (2): the box and the lid. The lid is a flat plate sitting on top.
Why “New”?
“New” makes a separate part. If you pick Add, OnShape glues the lid onto the box forever, and you’d have a box you can never open! 😅

6The lip (the tricky part!)

  1. Click Sketch, then click the top rim of the box again. (The lid is in the way? Click the 👁 eye next to the lid in the Parts list to hide it. Click it again later to show it.)
  2. Click Use and click the inside edges of the box (where the inside walls meet the rim).
  3. Click the Offset tool. Click the outline you just copied, and type 0.3. Make sure the new line is on the inside (click the flip arrow if it’s outside). That’s your gap.
  4. Use Offset again on the same copied outline, and type 1.8 (that’s the 0.3 gap + the 1.5 mm lip). Inside again! Click ✔.
  5. Click Extrude. Click only the thin ring between your two offset lines. That’s the lip.
  6. Choose Add at the top. Set Depth to 5 mm, going down into the box (flip it if needed).
  7. Turn on Merge scope and click the lid, so the lip joins the lid (not the box!). Click ✔.
Check
Show the lid and hide the box (eye icon in the Parts list). Flip the view to look under the lid: you should see the lip, a rounded rectangle ring sticking down 5 mm. Then show the box again.
Math check
Box inside = 60 − 2 − 2 = 56 mm long. Lip outside = 56 − 0.3 − 0.3 = 55.4 mm. That 0.6 mm of total wiggle room is what lets it fit!

7Make it yours 🎨

  • Change the size: right-click Sketch 1 → Edit, change 60 or 40, click ✔. Everything else updates by itself, even the lid! (Keep it under 80 mm.)
  • Put your initials on the lid: Sketch on the top of the lid, use the Text tool, then Extrude it Add 1 mm up (merge with the lid).
  • Make it deeper: edit Extrude 1 and change 30.

8Hand it in 📤

  1. Check the document name: Box – First Name Last Initial.
  2. Click Share, type your teacher’s email, choose Can view, turn on Export, and click Share.
  3. Tell your teacher it’s ready. The box and lid print as two separate parts, and the lid gets flipped over to print.
Teacher notes
Export each part separately (right-click the part in the Parts list → Export → STL). Print the lid upside down (flat top on the bed) so the lip needs no supports. If lids come out too tight on your printer, have students change both offsets: 0.4 / 1.9 instead of 0.3 / 1.8.

🔧 The fit test

When your box is printed, put the lid on. How does it feel? Circle one:

Too tight 😬     Just right 😃     Too loose 🙃

Too tight? Make the gap bigger (both offsets go up by 0.1). Too loose? Make it smaller. Then print the lid again. That’s called iterating, and real engineers do it all the time!

🤔 Think about it

1. Why did the lid need a gap? What would happen without it?

2. When you changed the box size in Sketch 1, the lid changed too. Why is that useful?
