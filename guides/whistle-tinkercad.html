Make a 3D Printed
Whistle in TinkerCad

Design a whistle that really works, and blow it for real. It prints in one piece!

How does a whistle work?

You blow air into a thin tunnel. The air shoots out and hits a sharp edge at the sound window. The air splits and makes the air inside the round chamber spin and shake really fast. Fast shaking air = sound!

Here’s the plan

You’ll build just the bottom half first: a round barrel plus a mouthpiece, scooped out with hole shapes. With the top off, you can see exactly what you’re making! Then you mirror it to make the top half and join them.

HoleA see-through shape. When you group it with a solid shape, it cuts that space away.
GroupJoins shapes into one. This is when the holes actually get cut.
AlignLines up the edges or centers of shapes so they fit together perfectly.
Name your design!
Your teacher downloads everyone’s whistles from TinkerCad to print them. Name yours Whistle – First Name Last Initial (like Whistle – Maya R) so we know it’s yours. You’ll do this in Step 1.
Golden rule
Made a mistake? Press Ctrl + Z to undo. You can’t break anything. 😄

How to read the sizes
Sizes go long × wide × tall. Looking from the top, “long” is left-right on your screen and “wide” is up-down. “Height off ground” is how high you lift the shape with the black cone. Keep this page next to you!
How to read the pictures
In the next pages, the newest part is bright and older parts are pale. Holes are blue here, but gray and stripy in TinkerCad. They stick up past the top of your half on purpose, so they cut all the way through. The colors on your screen will be different too, and that’s fine.

1Get set up

  1. Open TinkerCad and click Create → 3D Design.
  2. Name it right away! Click the silly random name at the top left, type Whistle – First Name Last Initial, and press Enter.
  3. Check that Snap Grid (bottom right) says 1.0 mm. Then click Top on the view cube so you’re looking straight down.

Two tricks you’ll use a lot

Typing a size: click a shape, hover over a white corner square, click a number that pops up, type the new number and press Enter. The height number is on the white square on top.
Lifting a shape: grab the black cone on top of the shape, click the number and type how high it goes.

2The barrel ①

  1. Drag a Cylinder onto the middle of the workplane.
  2. Make it 26 × 26 × 10. That’s half as tall as the finished whistle.

3The mouthpiece ②

  1. Drag a Box onto the workplane, to the left of the barrel and a little lower on your screen.
  2. Make it 26 × 6 × 10 (26 long, 6 wide, 10 tall).
  3. The box is still selected. Hold Shift and click the barrel, so both are selected.
  4. Click Align (or press L). Click the right dot in the left-right row, and the top dot in the up-down row (see the picture).
  5. Click the box by itself and press ← 13 times.
Click the two orange dots: right and top

4The hanger ring ③

  1. Drag a Cylinder onto the empty workplane and make it 8 × 8 × 10.
  2. Drag another Cylinder onto it, click Hole , and make it 4 × 4 × 12. Press D to drop it down to the workplane.
  3. Select both, Align both middle dots, then Group (Ctrl + G). Now it’s a ring!
  4. Drag the ring onto the barrel and press D to drop it down. Shift-click the barrel, Align both middle dots.
  5. Click the ring by itself and press → 15 times. It now sticks out the right side of the barrel.

5The chamber ④

  1. Drag a Cylinder onto the barrel. Click Hole. Make it 22 × 22 × 12.
  2. Shift-click the barrel, Align both middle dots.
  3. Click the hole by itself and set its height off the ground to 2. That leaves a floor 2 mm thick. The hole sticks up past the top of the barrel, and that’s just what we want!
Align tip
Align should move the new shape, not the big one. If the wrong shape moves, press Ctrl + Z, move the new shape so it sits more on top of the big one, and try again.

6The air tunnel ⑤ and sound window ⑥

These two holes are what make the whistle whistle, so be careful here!

  1. Air tunnel: drag a Box onto the mouthpiece, click Hole, and make it 28 × 2 × 12. Move it so it sticks out past the open (left) end, but not past the middle of the barrel.
  2. Shift-click the mouthpiece. Align: right dot and top dot.
  3. Click the tunnel by itself. Press ↓ 1 time, then set its height off the ground to 2.
  4. Sound window: drag a Cylinder onto the barrel, click Hole, and make it 9 × 9 × 12.
  5. Shift-click the barrel. Align both middle dots, just like the chamber.
  6. Click the new hole by itself. Press ← 2 times and ↑ 15 times, then set its height off the ground to 2.
Air tunnel dots: right and top
Count carefully!
The curve of this round hole is what carves the sharp edge that makes the sound. If it’s even a little off, the edge misses the air and you won’t get a whistle. Check your presses: ← 2 and ↑ 15. You’ll see the edge on the next page.

7Group your half and look inside

  1. Press Ctrl + A to select everything.
  2. Click Group (or Ctrl + G). The holes disappear and get cut out!
  3. Click Top on the view cube, then drag the view cube to tilt it a little, so you can look down inside.

How your whistle works

1. Your breath goes along the thin tunnel, like the straight arrow.

2. It hits the sharp edge and splits. Some escapes out of the window. The edge has to be sharp and thin: a thick, flat wall would just block the air, like blowing against your hand.

3. The rest spins around the chamber, like the dotted arrow, and shakes the air. That’s the sound!

Uh oh?
Doesn’t match the picture? Press Ctrl + Z to undo the group, then check the hole sizes, heights and arrow presses against the Parts Map. Fix it now, because the top half will be a copy of this one!

8Mirror it to make the top

  1. Click the view cube’s Home button (the little house) so you can see your half from the side. Click your half to select it.
  2. Duplicate: press Ctrl + D. It looks like nothing happened, but now there’s a copy sitting exactly on top of your half, and the copy is selected. Don’t click anywhere else!
  3. Mirror: press M. Arrows appear around the shape. Click the arrow that flips it upside down. Wrong arrow? Press Ctrl + Z and try another one.
  4. Stack: grab the black cone on top and set its height off the ground to 10. Now it sits right on top of Half 1.
  5. Join: press Ctrl + A, then Ctrl + G to group the two halves into one whistle.

9Final check ✅

  1. It’s one piece, and it looks like the Parts Map from the top: barrel, mouthpiece and hanger ring.
  2. There’s a thin slot at the end of the mouthpiece and a rounded window on the back, next to the barrel.
  3. It’s about 45 × 26 × 20 mm and sitting flat on the workplane.

10Save it and hand it in 💾

  1. Check the name at the top left says Whistle – First Name Last Initial. Not right? Click it and fix it.
  2. TinkerCad saves by itself while you work. To be sure, click the TinkerCad logo (top left) to go back to your designs. Your whistle should be there with your name on it.
  3. Tell your teacher your whistle is ready! Your teacher will download it from TinkerCad and 3D print it for you. 🖨️
  4. Done early? Make it yours! Add your initials on the side of the barrel with the Text shape (make it stick out just 1 mm), or try a different color. Don’t change the holes, or it might stop whistling!

🤔 Think about it

1. Which part of the whistle makes the sound? Explain it in your own words.

2. If you made another whistle, what would you change?
