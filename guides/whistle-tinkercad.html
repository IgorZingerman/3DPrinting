Make a 3D Printed
Whistle in TinkerCad

Design a whistle that really works, with a little “pea” rattling around inside it. It prints in one piece!

How does a whistle work?

You blow air into a thin tunnel. The air shoots out and hits a sharp edge at the sound window. The air splits and makes the air inside the round chamber spin and shake really fast. Fast shaking air = sound! The pea bounces around inside and makes the trilling “prrrrr” sound.

Here’s the plan

A whistle is a round barrel plus a mouthpiece, with the inside scooped out using hole shapes. You’ll build it from 7 simple parts, then group them into one whistle.

Hole
A see-through shape. When you group it with a solid shape, it cuts that space away.
Group
Joins shapes into one. This is when the holes actually get cut.
Align
Lines up the edges or centers of shapes so they fit together perfectly.
Name your design!
Your teacher downloads everyone’s whistles from TinkerCad to print them. Name yours Whistle – First Name Last Initial (like Whistle – Maya R) so we know it’s yours. You’ll do this in Step 1.
Golden rule
Made a mistake? Press Ctrl + Z to undo. You can’t break anything. 😄

How to read the sizes
Sizes go long × wide × tall. Looking from the top, “long” is left-right on your screen and “wide” is up-down. “Height off ground” is how high you lift the shape with the black cone. Keep this page next to you!

1Get set up

  1. Open TinkerCad and click Create → 3D Design.
  2. Name it right away! Click the silly random name at the top left, type Whistle – First Name Last Initial, and press Enter.
  3. Check that Snap Grid (bottom right) says 1.0 mm. Then click Top on the view cube so you’re looking straight down.

Two tricks you’ll use a lot

Typing a size: click a shape, hover over a white corner square, click a number that pops up, type the new number and press Enter. The height number is on the white square on top.
Lifting a shape: grab the black cone on top of the shape, click the number and type how high it goes.

2The barrel ①

  1. Drag a Cylinder onto the middle of the workplane.
  2. Make it 26 × 26 × 20.

3The mouthpiece ②

  1. Drag a Box onto the workplane, to the left of the barrel and a little lower on your screen.
  2. Make it 26 × 6 × 20 (26 long, 6 wide, 20 tall).
  3. The box is still selected. Hold Shift and click the barrel, so both are selected.
  4. Click Align (or press L). Click the right dot in the left-right row, and the top dot in the up-down row (see the picture).
  5. Click the box by itself and press ← 13 times.
Click the two orange dots: right and top
Check
Look at the Parts Map. The back of the mouthpiece lines up with the back of the barrel, and its right end stops at the middle of the barrel.

4The hanger ring ③

  1. Drag a Cylinder onto the empty workplane and make it 8 × 8 × 4.
  2. Drag another Cylinder onto it, click Hole , and make it 4 × 4 × 10. Press D to drop it down to the workplane.
  3. Select both, Align both middle dots, then Group (Ctrl + G). Now it’s a ring!
  4. Drag the ring onto the barrel and press D to drop it down. Shift-click the barrel, Align both middle dots.
  5. Click the ring by itself and press → 15 times. It now sticks out the back of the barrel.

5The chamber ④

  1. Drag a Cylinder onto the barrel. Click Hole. Make it 22 × 22 × 16.
  2. Shift-click the barrel, Align both middle dots.
  3. Click the hole by itself and set its height off the ground to 2. Now the barrel will have 2 mm thick walls all around.
Check
From the top, the ring sticks out the back of the barrel (the side away from the mouthpiece), and the chamber’s dashed circle sits right in the middle of the barrel with an even border all around.
Align tip
Align should move the new shape, not the big one. If the wrong shape moves, press Ctrl + Z, move the new shape so it sits more on top of the big one, and try again.

6The air tunnel ⑤ and sound window ⑥

These two little holes are what make the whistle whistle, so be careful here!

  1. Air tunnel: drag a Box onto the mouthpiece, click Hole, and make it 28 × 2 × 16. Move it so it sticks out past the open (left) end, but not past the middle of the barrel.
  2. Shift-click the mouthpiece. Align: right dot and top dot.
  3. Click the tunnel by itself. Press ↓ 1 time, then set its height off the ground to 2.
  4. Sound window: drag a Box onto the mouthpiece close to the barrel, click Hole, and make it 5 × 4 × 12.
  5. Shift-click the mouthpiece. Align: right dot and top dot.
  6. Click the window by itself. Press ↑ 1 time, then set its height off the ground to 4.
Same dots for both: right and top
Why does this work?
The tunnel is only 2 mm tall, so the air comes out as a thin, fast stream. The window sits right where the tunnel meets the barrel, so the stream hits the sharp edge there and splits in half.

7Group to cut the holes

  1. Press Ctrl + A to select everything.
  2. Click Group (or Ctrl + G).
Check
Click the view cube’s Home button (the little house) and drag the view cube to look around:
  • Look into the end of the mouthpiece: you should see a thin slot (the air tunnel).
  • On the flat top of the mouthpiece, right next to the barrel, you should see a rectangle opening (the sound window).
Uh oh?
No slot or no window? Press Ctrl + Z to undo the group, then check the hole sizes, heights and arrow presses against the Parts Map.

8Add the pea ⑦

The pea is printed inside the chamber, barely touching the floor. After printing, your teacher pokes it loose so it can rattle around. That’s why the pea is added after grouping, so the holes don’t cut it away!

  1. Drag a Sphere onto the whistle and make it 8 × 8 × 8.
  2. Shift-click the whistle. Align: right dot and middle dot.
  3. Click the pea by itself and press ← 15 times. Now it’s right above the chamber.
  4. Lift it to 1.9 (yes, one point nine). It sinks into the barrel and disappears. That’s correct!
  5. Press Ctrl + A, then Ctrl + G to group the pea with your whistle.
This time: right and middle
Why 1.9?
The chamber floor is 2 mm thick. Lifting the pea to 1.9 makes it sink just 0.1 mm into the floor. That tiny spot holds it in place while printing, and is easy to snap later.

9Final check ✅

  1. It looks like the Parts Map from the top: barrel, mouthpiece and hanger ring.
  2. There’s a thin slot at the end of the mouthpiece and a rectangle window next to the barrel.
  3. It’s about 39 × 26 × 20 mm and sitting flat on the workplane.

10Save it and hand it in 💾

  1. Check the name at the top left says Whistle – First Name Last Initial. Not right? Click it and fix it.
  2. TinkerCad saves by itself while you work. To be sure, click the TinkerCad logo (top left) to go back to your designs. Your whistle should be there with your name on it.
  3. Tell your teacher your whistle is ready! Your teacher will download it from TinkerCad and 3D print it for you. 🖨️
  4. Done early? Make it yours! Add your initials on the side of the barrel with the Text shape (make it stick out just 1 mm), or try a different color. Don’t change the holes, or it might stop whistling!
