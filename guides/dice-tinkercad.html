Make Your Own
Dice in TinkerCad

Design a real six-sided die with dots, then 3D print it and play a game with it!

Here’s the plan

A die is just a cube with little holes in it. In TinkerCad you make holes by putting “hole” shapes into a solid shape and then grouping them. We’ll do one side at a time, always working on the top of the die, then flip it over and do the next side. Easy!

You need
Your Chromebook and your TinkerCad class login
Sizes we’ll use
Die: 20 × 20 × 20 mm
Dots: 4 × 4 × 4 mm

Words to know

Hole
A see-through shape. When you group it with a solid shape, it cuts that space away.
Group
Joins shapes into one. This is the moment the holes actually get cut.
Align
Lines shapes up perfectly. We use it to put a dot in the exact center.
Name your design!
Your teacher downloads everyone’s dice from TinkerCad to print them. Name yours Dice – First Name Last Initial (like Dice – Maya R) so we know it’s yours. You’ll do this in Step 1.
Golden rule
Made a mistake? Press Ctrl + Z to undo. You can’t break anything. 😄

🗺️ The Dot Map

The secret rule of dice: opposite sides always add up to 7. So 1 is across from 6, 2 is across from 5, and 3 is across from 4.

Every dot starts in the center of the side. Then you move it with the arrow keys. Each press moves it 1 mm, so “5 →” means press the right arrow 5 times.

1Get set up

  1. Open TinkerCad and click Create → 3D Design.
  2. Name it right away! Click the silly random name TinkerCad gave your design (top left), type Dice – First Name Last Initial (like Dice – Maya R), and press Enter.
  3. In the bottom right corner, find Snap Grid. Make sure it says 1.0 mm.

2Make the die block

  1. Drag a red Box from Basic Shapes onto the workplane. It’s already 20 × 20 × 20 mm. 👍
  2. Optional, but it looks cool: in the Shape panel, slide Radius up to about 2 to round the corners like real dice.

3Make your first dot

  1. Drag a Sphere onto the workplane, next to the die.
  2. In the Shape panel, click Hole . The sphere turns see-through.
  3. Make it smaller: hover over a white corner square, click each number that pops up and type 4. You want 4 × 4 × 4.
  4. Lift it up: grab the little black cone on top of the dot, click the height number and type 18, then press Enter.

Why 18? The die is 20 mm tall. Lifting the 4 mm dot to 18 puts it halfway into the top, so it makes a nice round dimple.

Move it to the exact center

  1. Drag a box around the die and the dot so both are selected.
  2. Click Align (or press L). Small dots appear around your shapes.
  3. Click the middle dot on the line along the front, and the middle dot on the line along the side. Don’t click the up-and-down line, or your dot will lose its height!
Check
Your dot should sit right in the middle of the top, half sunk in. That’s side 1! 🎉

4Add the other dots

Side 1 only needs one dot, so you can skip this step the first time. For every other side, start with a fresh center dot (Step 3), then:

  1. Click Top on the view cube (the little cube in the top left) so you’re looking straight down. Now the arrow keys go the same way as the arrows on the Dot Map.
  2. Click the center dot and press Ctrl + D (Duplicate). A copy appears right on top of it.
  3. Use the arrow keys to move the copy. Check the Dot Map for how many presses. Example for dot A on side 4: press ← 5 times and ↑ 5 times.
  4. Repeat until you have every dot for that side.
  5. If this side has no middle dot (sides 2, 4 and 6), click the center dot and press Backspace.
Tip
Click the view cube’s Home button (the little house) any time to see the die from the side again. Look closely: all the dots should be at the same height.

5Group to cut the holes

  1. Press Ctrl + A to select everything (the die and this side’s dots).
  2. Click Group (or press Ctrl + G).
Check
The see-through spheres disappear and you have real dimples in the top of your die. 🙌 The die is now one shape, so it’s safe to flip it.
Uh oh?
If a dot went to the wrong place, press Ctrl + Z to undo the group, fix the dot, then group again.

6Flip it and do the next side

We always add dots to the top. So after each side, we turn the die until a blank side is on top. Do the sides in this order and the opposite sides will always add up to 7 automatically!

How to turn the die

  1. Click the die. You’ll see three curved arrows. Two are on the sides (those flip it over). The one near the bottom just spins it in place, so don’t use that one.
  2. Drag a side arrow around until the number says the angle in the chart below. You can also click the number and type it.
  3. If the die ends up floating or sunk into the workplane, press D to drop it back down.

Then for each side: Turn ➜ Make a center dot (Step 3) ➜ Add dots (Step 4) ➜ Group (Step 5) ➜ repeat!

7Final check ✅

Click the view cube’s Home button, then drag the view cube to spin around and look at every side. Check each box:

  1. All six sides have dots: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6.
  2. Opposite sides add up to 7 (1 & 6, 2 & 5, 3 & 4).
  3. All the dots are dimples (holes), not bumps sticking out.
  4. It’s still about 20 × 20 × 20 mm and sitting flat on the workplane.

8Save it and hand it in 💾

  1. Check the name at the top left says Dice – First Name Last Initial. Not right? Click it and fix it.
  2. TinkerCad saves by itself while you work. To be sure, click the TinkerCad logo (top left) to go back to your designs. Your die should be there with your name on it.
  3. Tell your teacher your die is ready! Your teacher will download it from TinkerCad and 3D print it for you. 🖨️
  4. Done early? Make it yours! Try a different color, rounder corners, or a tiny shape on side 1 instead of a dot (like a star or your initial). Keep the dots at least 3 mm wide so they show up when printed.

🤔 Think about it

Real designers ask themselves these questions after every project.

1. What was the hardest part, and how did you figure it out?

2. If you made another die, what would you do differently?
