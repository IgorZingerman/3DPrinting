Getting Started
with OnShape

OnShape is the 3D design tool real engineers use. It runs right in your Chromebook’s browser, no download needed!

Do this in class, with your teacher
Make your account in class, while your teacher is watching. If you’re under 13, OnShape needs a grown-up (your teacher) to say it’s OK, so don’t sign up at home on your own.

1Make your account

  1. Go to onshape.com/education and click Sign up (it’s free for students).
  2. Choose Student, then fill in the form with your school email and your school’s name. Ask your teacher if you’re not sure what to put.
  3. Open your school email. Find the email from OnShape and click the link inside to activate your account. No email after 5 minutes? Check your spam folder, then tell your teacher.
  4. Make a password. Use one you’ll remember, and never share it with anyone (except a grown-up at home).

2Sign in

  1. Go to cad.onshape.com and sign in with your school email and password.
  2. Bookmark it! Press Ctrl + D so you can find it fast next time.
Teacher notes
Students under 13 need a qualified adult (school, teacher or parent) to create or authorize the account. Per OnShape, a school or educator can consent on parents’ behalf for school use, following district policy. There’s no bulk sign-up on the free Education plan, so each student signs up separately. If activation emails don’t arrive, ask IT to allow email from onshape.com.

🗺️ A tour of the screen

Click Create → Document (top left), name it Practice – First Name Last Initial, and click Create. Here’s what you’ll see:

①Document menu (☰) and name: click the name to rename your document.
②Toolbar: the tools that make shapes. Hover over any button to see its name.
③Feature list: every step you do is saved here, in order. Right-click a step and choose Edit to change it later. Super handy!
④Parts list: the finished parts you made.
⑤View cube: click a side (like Top or Front) to look from that side.
⑥Graphics area: where your design shows up.
⑦Tabs: you’ll work in Part Studio 1.

One-time setup: millimeters

  1. Click the Document menu (☰) → Workspace units. Set Length to Millimeter and click OK. Do this in every new document, so 20 means 20 mm, not 20 inches!

🖱️ Moving around on a Chromebook

🔄Spin the view: press down on the touchpad with two fingers and drag. Or click the arrows around the view cube.
🔍Zoom: slide two fingers up or down on the touchpad (without pressing).
🎯Lost? Press F to zoom to fit. Your whole design jumps back into view.
📐Look straight at something: click Top, Front or Right on the view cube.
↩️Undo: Ctrl + Z, just like in TinkerCad.
⎋Stuck in a tool? Press Esc to stop.

📤 How to hand in your work

OnShape saves everything by itself, all the time. To hand in a project, you share it with your teacher:

  1. Make sure your document is named Project – First Name Last Initial (like Top – Maya R).
  2. Click Share (top right).
  3. Type your teacher’s email: iggyzs@yahoo.com
  4. Choose Can view, and turn on Export (so your teacher can download it to print). Click Share.

How is OnShape different from TinkerCad?
In TinkerCad you stack ready-made shapes. In OnShape you draw a flat sketch first, then turn it into 3D (by pulling it up, or spinning it around). It’s how real products get designed!
