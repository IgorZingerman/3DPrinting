Spinning Top
in OnShape 🌀

Draw half of a top, spin it around a line, and OnShape makes the whole 3D top for you!

The big idea: Revolve

A top is perfectly round. So instead of building it piece by piece, you draw the outline of one half (like a top cut in half), and the Revolve tool spins that drawing all the way around a center line. It’s like a pottery wheel! 🏺

What makes a top spin a long time?
  • Wide and heavy near the bottom: a wide disc keeps spinning, like a flywheel.
  • A sharp, centered tip: less rubbing on the table.
  • A short handle: just long enough to twist with your fingers.
The 3D printer rule
Your top prints upside down, handle on the bed. The printer can’t print in mid-air, so slanted parts need to be steep (no flatter than 45°). That’s why the starter design slants up to the handle instead of being flat.
Before you start
You need your OnShape account (see Getting Started with OnShape). Make a new document named Top – First Name Last Initial, and set Workspace units to Millimeter.

This is the half you’ll draw. The red center line is where it spins. Each letter is a size you’ll type in.

ADisc, center to edge20
BTip cone height15
CDisc edge5
DHandle, center to edge4
EHandle length14
FTotal height50

All sizes in mm. The finished top is 40 mm wide (2 × A) and 50 mm tall.

Why only half the width?
You only draw from the center line out to the edge. When it spins around, the other half appears by itself. So A = 20 makes a disc that’s 40 mm across!

1Start a sketch

  1. Click Sketch in the toolbar, then click the Front plane (in the graphics area or the feature list).
  2. OnShape turns the view so you’re looking straight at the plane. If it doesn’t, press N (it means “look straight at my sketch”).

2Draw the outline

Use the Line tool (L). Click once at each corner. Don’t worry about exact sizes yet, just get the shape right!

  1. Click on the origin (the dot in the middle). That’s the tip of your top.
  2. Click up and to the right (the slanted cone), then straight up a little (the disc edge).
  3. Click up and to the left (the slant toward the handle), then straight up (the handle).
  4. Click straight left, so you land right above the origin. Then click back on the origin to close the shape.
  5. Press Esc. If the inside of your shape turns gray/shaded, it’s closed. 🎉 If not, a corner isn’t connected: drag the loose end onto the point it should touch.
Straight lines
When a line is perfectly up-down or left-right, OnShape shows a little | or — symbol next to it. You want those on the edge, handle, top and center lines!

3Add the sizes

  1. Click Dimension (D). Click a line, move your mouse to place the number, and click. Type the size and press Enter.
  2. To size A and D: click the center line, then the edge line, and place it above.
  3. To size B: click the origin, then the corner at the end of the cone. Move your mouse to the side so the number measures up-and-down.
  4. Do all six: A 20, B 15, C 5, D 4, E 14, F 50. Then click the green ✔ to finish the sketch.

4Revolve it!

  1. Click Revolve in the toolbar.
  2. For Faces and sketch regions, click inside your shaded shape.
  3. For Revolve axis, click the center line (the line going straight up from the origin).
  4. Make sure it says Full, then click the green ✔. Spin the view around… it’s a top! 🌀
Uh oh?
Revolve won’t work or looks weird? You probably picked the wrong line as the axis. Right-click Revolve 1 in the feature list, choose Edit, and pick the center line again.

5Round the sharp edges (optional)

  1. Click Fillet. Click the sharp edges around the disc. Set the radius to 1 mm and click ✔.
  2. Don’t round the tip too much. A sharp tip spins longer!

6Make it yours 🎨

Now experiment! Right-click Sketch 1 → Edit, change a size, and click ✔. The whole top updates by itself.

  • Make the disc wider or narrower (keep the whole top under 60 mm wide).
  • Try a curvy outline with the Tangent arc tool instead of straight lines.
  • Remember the printer rule: no slants flatter than 45°, and no overhanging ledges.

7Hand it in 📤
